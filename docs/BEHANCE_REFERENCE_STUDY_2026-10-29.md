## Scope and evidence

Research and library organisation for developing the individual portfolio projects. No website templates, project content, runtime assets, deployment settings or GitHub branches were changed. The 35 represented references below were reviewed from the supplied local artwork, not merely page titles or remote search snippets.

**Coverage:** 67 listed references; 35 represented; 32 without matching local captures. The upload has 199 design screenshots, 1 incidental Touch Bar screenshot and 50 videos (257.06 seconds total). Every PNG was visually inspected on ordered contact sheets; selected reference pages were also opened at full size for hierarchy, text and material details. This is a visual/design study of supplied excerpts. It does not establish full projects beyond the captured sections, exact motion easing, frame-by-frame timing or audio. ## What makes the strongest projects convincing

1. **One organising idea produces many assets.** Midday’s modular language, RESIDUE’s R, ANAHÍ’s craft/floral symbol and BRUMO’s sharing vocabulary provide rules that survive several formats. Additional artwork should deepen a system.
2. **Applications have different jobs.** A front pack attracts; a back label informs; a menu helps selection; a receipt/order seal completes a transaction; a campaign poster communicates a single idea. Repeating a hero logo on unrelated objects does not demonstrate this range.
3. **The work moves between scales.** Start with a compelling finished scene, then explain the visual system, show complete flat artwork, crop into material/type details, and return to the object in use. Flat artwork and contextual mockups validate each other.
4. **Hierarchy supplies coherence.** Stable spacing, wordmark placement and product naming can support substantial variation in image, colour or headline. A series should be recognisable without every piece using an identical template.
5. **People and materials carry the brand.** Hands, eating rituals, hair, paper, fabric and light are relevant evidence. Choose art direction that makes sense for the subject.
6. **Motion belongs to the identity.** Useful examples change a mark’s construction, demonstrate an application family or reveal material. Keep a readable resting state and animate a limited region when the surrounding composition already explains the project.
7. **Selection matters more than volume.** A weaker repeated tile dilutes an otherwise strong case. Each new asset must demonstrate a different design decision, use or scale.
8. **Process must be truthful.** We can create and label new concept development; we cannot backfill invented historic research, client approvals, production, launch results or performance metrics.

## Reference-by-reference observations

### 1.01 Ramen Restaurant Branding Visual Identity

[Source project](https://www.behance.net/gallery/250881213/Ramen-Restaurant-Branding-Visual-Identity) · 3 PNG / 0 MOV · library `01 Food, restaurant branding/01 Ramen Restaurant Branding Visual Identity`

**Observed:** Bowl & Chill: saturated blue/red/cream, a bowl-led signature, comic-like lettering, a small illustration family, packaging and phone/social compositions.

**Lesson:** The restaurant is recognisable through several assets, not one lockup; energetic food visuals are balanced by quieter system explanations.

**Relevance:** Beans Central: derive a small original food/utensil graphic family from the supplied icon and use it consistently on packaging and campaign layouts.

## Motion interpretation

The recordings are short captures of portions of long project presentations, frequently with several independently animated panels. The visible “Record Selected Portion” overlay is capture UI, not a brand element. Do not reproduce it. Several recordings hold on almost-static content; not every MOV demonstrates a meaningful animation.

- CluckRiot: small mark/type and application-panel changes within a hot-colour layout.
- Cozy Loop/HREHEN: design system demonstrations through background/type or mark-state changes.
- Crochetree/Crochet Stories/ANAHÍ/Common Thread: alternating illustration, poster, care and event compositions.
- DUET/Candeur/Cosmomedica: contained campaign changes, type specimen/letter changes and identity reveals.
- SILVERA: the richest recorded sequence here; material/product reveals and transitions between model, mark, print and object imagery.
- Paralian/BABASITA: changing art-directed tiles, typography and product contexts.
- JAMMY/Calvé/Pasty: variant sequencing, word/letter transformations, graphic repetition and stable-layout campaign swaps.
- MORMOR: a multi-moment brand film embedded in the presentation alongside a restrained mark sequence.
- Peppa Please/Midday/BRUMO: compact colour/wordmark or application switches; the still artwork remains the main story.

Exact easing, timings and micro-transitions should be re-examined at playback speed when producing a specific animation. Our own motion should use original vector/typographic assets with a legible still alternative.
